01

Overview and goals

Why this system is being built, and how we will know it worked.

calendar-buddy is a lightweight, fast calendar application for Linux, connected directly to Google Calendar. It replaces opening Google Calendar in the browser for everyday calendar work. It is built for the product owner's own use, with the option of sharing it with colleagues, and runs without a server of its own: the data stays in Google and in a local cache.

Goals

G1

For two consecutive working weeks, the product owner checks upcoming meetings, responds to invitations and creates meetings with Google Meet only in the app, without opening Google Calendar in the browser.

G2

All ten acceptance criteria of the PRD (§14) hold in daily use.

G3

Start-up, switching period or view, and scrolling the week view stay within the thresholds set after the first measurements, on a profile of 5 accounts, 50 calendars and about 20,000 cached events.

G4

The app installs from a .deb package and works on Ubuntu 26.10 with KDE Plasma, on Wayland and on X11.

Glossary

Terms with one fixed meaning across the project.

TermMeans exactly
AccountA Google account (personal or Workspace) connected to the app.
CalendarA Google calendar accessible to a connected account, including read-only and subscribed ones.
Primary calendarAn account's main calendar; invitations land there, and on a Workspace account only there can special event types be created.
Default calendarThe calendar, chosen by the user, that new events go to.
Hidden calendarA calendar whose events are temporarily hidden by the quick toggle; it stays listed and keeps synchronizing.
Disabled calendarA calendar turned off in the settings; it is not listed, not synchronized and gives no reminders.
Muted calendarA calendar whose notifications are off; its events stay visible.
EventA single entry in a Google calendar.
Series / occurrenceA recurring event / one of its instances.
Edit scopeThe reach of a change to a series: this event, this and following, or all.
Special event typeOut of office, focus time, working location or birthday.
Organizer / GuestThe person who invites to an event / an invited person, required or optional.
InvitationAn event in which the user is a guest.
ResponseA guest's answer: Accept, Decline or Maybe (Google's "tentative").
ReminderA per-user Google reminder on an event, as a notification or an e-mail, synchronized with Google.
NotificationThe desktop notification the app shows for a notification-type reminder.
CacheThe local copy of calendar data, from one year back to two years ahead.
OfflineNo network; only what is in the cache can be browsed.
Free/busy previewGuests' busy blocks for the chosen day, shown in the event editor.
JoinOpening the Google Meet meeting of an event from the event, the agenda or a notification.
04

Scope

What we deliver and — just as important — what we deliberately do not.

In scope

  • Google accounts and sign-in — several personal and Workspace accounts through the browser, refresh and revoked access, account removal with its data, first run
  • Calendars, synchronisation and offline cache — all accessible calendars in one view, local colours, background sync (about a minute) and on demand, cache from one year back to two years ahead, offline browsing, read-only calendars
  • Views and navigation — day, 3 days, week, month, agenda; side panel grouped by account; hiding and disabling calendars; keyboard; regional formats and time zone from the system
  • Visual direction and themes — Morgen and Apple Calendar as references, light and dark themes, English interface
  • Creating and editing events — quick entry and full editor, dragging, recurrence presets with three edit scopes, special event types, event colours, attachments as links
  • Guests, invitations, RSVP and Google Meet — guest suggestions (contacts, Workspace directory, recent), responses, asking before notifying guests, free/busy preview, Meet with Join
  • Reminders and notifications — notifications from Google reminders, editing reminders, snooze, muting calendars, tray and autostart, single instance, webcal: links

Out of scope

  • Providers other than Google — CalDAV, Microsoft 365 / Outlook.
  • macOS and Windows releases; public distribution, Google verification and choosing a licence.
  • Offline editing with a change queue and conflict resolution.
  • Calendar management — creating, renaming, deleting, sharing, administration.
  • Event search, basic or advanced.
  • A workweek view; a second time zone or choosing the display time zone by hand.
  • Advanced theming and layout customization, interface translations, mock-ups.
  • Natural-language event creation, task management, an AI scheduling assistant.
  • Adding Google Drive attachments; ICS import and export.
  • A full recurrence-rule editor; creating working-location events.
  • Advanced scheduling with guests (multi-day grid, free-slot suggestions); responding with a comment or proposing another time.
  • App-level default reminders and local-only reminders.
  • Handling mailto: links; update checking and automatic updates.
  • Telemetry and remote crash reports; screen-reader support; package formats other than .deb; a fallback token store when no secret store runs.

Product, roles, platform and integrationsDSC-01 · cross-cutting

What the product is and for whom (the single end user, product framing, the "replace the browser" goal), the Google integration strategy (Calendar API, People API, OAuth verification), the provider abstraction and future providers, the target platforms, and the preliminary stack (see SRC-01 Noted: §10.1, §10.2, §15, §16). Ends where a business topic describes behaviour. Project-level questions with no other home (acceptance, milestones, support) live here.

DSC-01-01

A lightweight, fast desktop calendar application, built primarily for Linux.

DSC-01-02

Google Calendar is the first and only fully supported provider, integrated through the Google Calendar API rather than CalDAV.

DSC-01-03

Contact suggestions come from the Google People API; Workspace directory suggestions depend on extra permissions and are not available for every account.

DSC-01-04

The app talks to Google directly from the user's computer; there is no sync backend of our own.

DSC-01-05

Calendar providers are kept separate from the app's own model of calendars and events, and provider-specific features are optional, so CalDAV or Microsoft 365 can be added later without reshaping the app.

DSC-01-06

Platform-specific behaviour sits behind interfaces, so macOS and Windows can come later from the same codebase.

DSC-01-07

Google's OAuth verification is needed only before a public distribution of the app.

DSC-01-08

For now the app is for the product owner's own use; it may be shared with teammates in the same Google Workspace organization, and no public release is planned.

DSC-01-09

There is one kind of user: a person signed in with their own Google account, acting as organizer or attendee; the app has no admin role.

DSC-01-10

The MVP is built and tested on Linux only; the architecture keeps macOS and Windows possible, but nothing is built or tested for them.

DSC-01-13

"Everyday" calendar work — the work the app must take over from the browser — is checking upcoming meetings, accepting invitations and creating meetings with Google Meet; opening the browser for advanced operations is acceptable.

DSC-01-14

Delivery goes straight to the MVP, with no separate prototype milestone and no fixed dates.

DSC-01-15

The product owner accepts the MVP against the PRD's acceptance criteria through daily use; support and maintenance are not in scope.

DSC-01-16

There is no server infrastructure: the only environment is the GCP project in the organization's Google Workspace, owned and paid for by the organization and administered by the product owner.

DSC-01-17

Google access goes through an External OAuth app in production status, without Google verification: any Google account — personal or organization, several at once — can sign in; users see Google's "unverified app" warning once, and the app is capped at 100 users until it is verified before a public release.

DSC-01-18

The UI toolkit is chosen by a feasibility check in the first stories, starting with egui; Iced is the fallback.

Non-functional requirementsDSC-02 · cross-cutting

Performance, robustness, security of tokens and logs, test automation, Linux compatibility (Wayland/X11, DEs, packaging), accessibility and the desktop-native qualities. Works through the technical checklist (TQ01–TQ24) into TECH-CHECKLIST.md.

DSC-02-01

The app keeps responding while it synchronizes with Google or waits for the network.

DSC-02-02

It uses little CPU when idle and modest memory, starts fast and switches views fast, also with many events.

DSC-02-03

Concrete performance thresholds come from early measurements, not assumptions.

DSC-02-04

The app copes with network failures, Google API limits, and expired or revoked account access.

DSC-02-05

Tokens are kept in the system credential store, and the app asks Google only for the minimum permissions.

DSC-02-06

Errors are reported clearly, and logs contain no sensitive user data.

DSC-02-07

Recurring events behave correctly across time zones and daylight-saving changes.

DSC-02-08

The app behaves like a native Linux application — system notifications, keyboard operation, HiDPI, window resizing, the system theme — on Wayland and X11 in mainstream desktop environments.

DSC-02-09

The MVP's accessibility target is full keyboard operation, readable contrast in both themes and correct HiDPI scaling; screen-reader support is not an MVP goal.

DSC-02-10

The MVP is tested on Ubuntu 26.10 with KDE Plasma, mainly on Wayland with an X11 check, and ships as a .deb package; other package formats come after the MVP.

DSC-02-11

There is no telemetry and no remote crash reporting; logs are local, in the user's directory, with a user-set level, and never contain event contents or tokens.

DSC-02-12

Performance is measured on a profile of up to 5 accounts, 50 calendars and about 20,000 cached events, for start-up, switching period or view, and scrolling the week view; the product owner sets the thresholds after the first measurements.

DSC-02-13

The app's only network traffic is HTTPS to Google's APIs.

Google accounts and sign-inDSC-03 · business area

Connecting Google accounts (several at once, personal and Workspace), signing in through the browser, keeping credentials safe, refreshing and re-authenticating, removing an account, and telling account problems apart from sync problems. Ends where calendars are fetched (DSC-04).

DSC-03-01

The user connects a Google account by signing in through their own browser; nothing — no server, no password — is typed into the app.

DSC-03-02

Several Google accounts, personal and organization, can be connected at once.

DSC-03-03

Once signed in, the user stays signed in — the app refreshes access on its own; if Google revokes it, the app says so plainly and offers to sign in again instead of silently stopping.

DSC-03-04

The user can add an account, remove it, and sign in again to an existing one.

DSC-03-05

A problem with an account (signed out, access revoked) is shown separately from a problem synchronizing a single calendar.

DSC-03-06

On first run with no account the user sees a welcome screen with one action — connect a Google account; the calendar appears after the first sync.

DSC-03-07

With no network on first run, signing in is impossible; the app says so and lets the user retry once the network is back.

DSC-03-08

Removing an account immediately deletes its tokens, its cached calendars and events, and its local settings from the device; the Google account itself is untouched.

Calendars, synchronisation and offline cacheDSC-04 · business area

Which calendars appear and how (all accessible calendars, unified across accounts, visibility and colours), keeping them in sync automatically and on demand, the local cache, offline browsing and recovery from errors, and the sync state shown to the user. Ends where events are drawn (DSC-05) or changed (DSC-07).

DSC-04-01

The app fetches every calendar the connected accounts can access.

DSC-04-02

Calendars from all accounts are shown together, in one view.

DSC-04-03

Each calendar can be shown or hidden; it takes its colour from Google by default, and the user can change it locally without changing it in Google.

DSC-04-04

Changes synchronize on their own in the background, and the user can also refresh by hand; each time, only what changed is fetched where Google allows it.

DSC-04-05

Calendar data is kept locally, so the app opens fast and previously synchronized events can be browsed without network.

DSC-04-06

Synchronization recovers on its own from lost network, expired sync state, Google's limits and transient errors.

DSC-04-07

The synchronization state is visible and never blocks work; after a start without network the app shows the calendar from the cache with a clear "offline" status.

DSC-04-08

Events show at the right local time across time zones and daylight-saving changes, including moved or cancelled occurrences of a series.

DSC-04-09

The app works with calendars that already exist in Google; creating, renaming and deleting calendars happens in the browser.

DSC-04-10

Read-only and subscribed calendars (holidays, birthdays, colleagues') are shown like the others; their events cannot be edited, and that is clearly visible; free/busy-only calendars show busy blocks without details.

DSC-04-11

The cache holds events from one year back to two years ahead; older or later periods browsed online are fetched from Google as needed.

DSC-04-12

When a calendar stops being accessible while its account still works, it disappears from the app with its cached events, and the user is told once.

DSC-04-13

Without network the user can only browse; creating, editing and responding to invitations are unavailable, and the app says why.

DSC-04-14

While the app is open, changes made elsewhere appear within about a minute, and right after the computer wakes or the network returns.
